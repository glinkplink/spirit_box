# Mileage tracker niche — KILL or ADVANCE

**Date captured:** 2026-09-10  
**Role:** Kill-or-advance ledger for the provisional IRS-ready mileage-tracker niche. Supporting research. Does **not** override `docs/00_SPIRIT_BOX_PRODUCT_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`. Does **not** authorize a build.  
**Evidence labels:** VERIFIED FACT / INFERENCE / SPECULATION / UNKNOWN.  
**Method:** Public iTunes Search / Lookup / RSS (US unless noted), Magica site and App Store listings, Play Store listing, web search. iTunes Search order is **not** a guaranteed on-device SERP clone (same caveat as the 2026-09-10 Spirit Box revalidation). **P0 natural experiment:** Magica (`Mileage Tracker - Magica Car`, id `1341304115`) already ships the wedge we were considering.

# 1. VERDICT

**KILL.**

There is real anger at MileIQ’s 2026 price hike, and a minority of recent 1–3★ reviewers say they will cancel or look elsewhere. That is **not** evidence that an unknown solo iOS developer has a credible U.S. acquisition path.

Magica has operated since 2018 with automatic detection, IRS-ready exports, on-device/privacy positioning, CarPlay + Watch, $14.99/year and $69.99 lifetime, a solo developer, claimed Apple featuring, a website, localized listings, and a 2026 “best MileIQ alternatives” SEO article aimed at the exact switcher we hoped to intercept. After eight years it has **19 U.S. ratings (3.89★)**. Italy, where it is titled *Gestione auto - Magica*, has **176 ratings**. The U.S. Play listing shows a **100+** install bucket.

Advance required **both** active switching demand **and** evidence a small unknown entrant can access users. The first is weak (most recent MileIQ reviews are still 5★; named destinations are TripLog/QuickBooks, not indies). The second fails: Magica is the best available test of “cheap + private + IRS-ready + solo,” and it did not break out in the U.S.

Do not build. Do not continue mileage research. Resume niche discovery (acreage or the next candidate). Spirit Box remains **CONDITIONAL BUILD**; this ledger does not change it.

**INFERENCE (supported by the mix, not by a survey):** The 2026 review storm is **primarily a price-hike revolt among people who already accepted automatic tracking**, with a **large secondary cluster who say the automatic product is failing them**. It is not “users love MileIQ’s GPS and only hate the bill.” It is also not “the product is universally broken.” **74.6% of this 500-review window are still 5★.**

Illustrative 1★/3★ that love the job and hate the price:

- “I love this app but with it going to $140 a year it’s time to move on.”
- “In 3 years of using it, it’s never missed a drive… But they are raising the subscription from $89.99 to $139.99.”
- “The price is worth the amount of deductions I get” appears in **5★** copy; several **4★** say “expensive but it works.”

Illustrative 1★ that are product-failure, not price:

- “Paid for a full year… it just stopped tracking… around tax time there was no way to recover the last 6 months.”
- “Misses 40% of my drives.”
- Realtor: “It cuts off and doesn’t consistently track my mileage.”
- Delivery: “misses more than half if the app is not open… 130 stops.”

**Bending Spoons:** Magica’s own June 2026 alternatives article attributes the hike to the July 2025 Bending Spoons acquisition ($5.99 → $8.99 → $13.99/month). **VERIFIED FACT** that users in this RSS window talk about **Microsoft** and dollar amounts, not the new owner’s name. Acquisition-as-a-complaint-theme is real but secondary to the number on the renewal email.

**Battery:** Magica users (US + IT) *do* complain about GPS/battery. MileIQ’s angry U.S. window in this sample does not. Do not import Magica’s battery problem into MileIQ’s review census.

# 8. Engineering implication

Table stakes for this job, from reviews across MileIQ, Magica, Mileage IQ, Mile AI:

- Background drive detection that does not silently die after a phone swap or iOS update.
- Not missing short trips, 130-stop delivery days, or remote areas.
- Classification that does not require 130 opens; work/personal that does not mangle school drop-off.
- Battery/location permissions that iOS will not let you honestly “turn off except in the car” without BLE hardware (Magica’s own developer reply states this Apple limitation).
- Tax-season support load when a month of miles vanishes.

That is a **location-background product with IRS liability**, not an offline audio instrument. It is heavier than Spirit Box V1 (local sweep, no account, no GPS).

If the only way to take switchers is “more reliable than MileIQ,” **do not enter**. Magica’s Italian 1★s show even a dedicated car-app author has not closed that loop.

# 11. Kill criterion

All seven listed kill criteria are **substantially true**:

1. **Price complaints common, users do not actually switch (to us).** 48/101 1–3★ mention price; 65/101 have **no** switching language; named exits are TripLog/QuickBooks; 373/500 recent reviews are 5★.
2. **Small apps remain commercially useless on useful terms.** They can **appear**; they do not **accumulate U.S. ratings**. Magica is absent from those SERPs.
3. **Magica already solves price/privacy/IRS and has negligible U.S. traction.** 19 U.S. ratings, 176 IT, Play 100+.
4. **Required differentiation for people who would leave is reliable automatic detection.** That is technically and support intensive. Penalize.
5. **Long tails are low-intent, contaminated, or title-stuffed.** `schedule c` is calendars; `realtor mileage` is Trulia; `mileiq alternative` is clones under MileIQ #1. Volume unknown and not needed.
6. **Acquisition that Magica already tried** (SEO including a MileIQ-alternatives article, Reddit, featuring claims, localization, eight years of updates) **did not produce a U.S. business.** Repeating that as “group posting / content / paid UA” would be the same job with less of a product.
7. **Accessible economics do not beat Spirit Box’s modest bar.** Thousands of annual seats required for $10k/mo; no evidence of an on-ramp to hundreds of U.S. ratings, let alone thousands of seats.
