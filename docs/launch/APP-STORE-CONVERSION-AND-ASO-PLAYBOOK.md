## Why this is stronger than the working package

**VERIFIED FACT:** Apple gives only 30 characters to the app name and 30 to the subtitle. The first one to three portrait screenshots can appear in search results when no App Preview is present. Apple explicitly advises using those first screenshots to convey the essence of the app. [A1][A2]

**VERIFIED FACT:** The current May-2026 leader, `Spirit Box SBX Ghost Talker`, uses a literal handheld-radio image as its icon and a hardware-inspired first screenshot headed “Classic Design Reimagined.” Its current U.S. listing has 49 ratings at 2.9 stars. [W1][W2]

**INFERENCE:** We do not need to out-SB7 the current leader. It already owns the most literal “hardware replica” visual cue. Our conversion advantage is a more credible **instrument workflow**: spirit box → MARK → exact replay, with a better trust and purchase story.

**RECOMMENDATION:** Spend the title after `Spirit Box` on the differentiating behavior (`Sweep & Replay`), not another generic paranormal term. Spend the subtitle on the two strongest complementary benefits (`Mark Moments` + `Works Offline`). This gives the search result four distinct reasons to click without repeating metadata.

## Main commercial risk

Our listing can still fail if **MARK does not read as valuable without explanation**. “MARK” is not self-evident to a new casual user. The solution is not to rename the feature. The solution is to make screenshots 2 and 3 a two-panel cause-and-effect story:

> **Hear something? Tap MARK. → Replay the exact moment.**

GhostTube EVP already proves that tagging moments inside paranormal recordings is a legible investigator workflow; its current product explicitly promotes audio tagging, scrubbable review, and instant playback. [E1][E2]

## Cheapest pre-asset test

Before final screenshot production, place the proposed icon + first three rough comps beside current competitor search results at real App Store thumbnail scale. Show them to target users for five seconds without explanation. Ask only:

1. What kind of app is this?
2. What is different about it?
3. What do you think MARK does?

Do not tell them the intended answer.

### Redesign criterion

Redesign before launch if repeated answers are:
- “voice recorder,” “radio app,” or “EMF meter” instead of spirit box;
- “I don’t know what MARK means” after seeing screenshots 2 and 3;
- “looks like an SB7 clone” or users identify a specific physical product from the icon/UI;
- “ghost game/prank app” rather than instrument.

# 5. FINAL ICON CREATIVE BRIEF

## What current icons teach us

**VERIFIED FACT:** The current leader uses a near-photographic handheld radio/device with a bright red display and red glow. It reads “hardware” instantly, but it also pushes close to a specific physical-device association. [W1]

**VERIFIED FACT:** Necrophonic uses a stark monochrome gear/pentagram icon. It is memorable and distinctive, but it communicates occult brand identity more than `spirit box`. [N1][N2]

**VERIFIED FACT:** GhostTube VOX uses a black icon with a simple white ghost/waveform mark and `VOX`, leveraging established brand recognition and an obvious paranormal/audio cue. [G1][G2]

**VERIFIED FACT:** Spirit Entities Talker uses a red neon hooded/spectral figure. Spirit Talker uses prominent text over a spectral face/waveform. Ghost Hunting Tools uses a cracked-glass handprint. These are high-paranormal cues, but they read closer to horror/ghost entertainment than a focused instrument. [S1][T1][H1]

## Concept-family comparison

| Concept family | Tiny-size recognition | Uniqueness | Professionalism | Paranormal relevance | Generic audio/radio risk | Game/horror risk | Trade-dress risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Abstract instrument display** | **Very high** | High | **Very high** | High | Medium | **Low** | **Low if abstracted** | **WINNER** |
| Handheld device abstraction | High | Medium | High | High | Medium | Low | **High** | Reject |
| Sweep waveform | High | Medium | High | Medium | **Very high** | Low | Low | Reject |
| Signal bars | High | Medium | High | Low-medium | **Very high** | Low | Low | Reject |
| Physical control close-up | Medium | Medium | High | Medium | Medium | Low | High | Reject |
| Minimal technical symbol | Medium-low | High | Very high | Low | High | Low | Low | Reject |

## Final icon direction

### Core composition

Create a **1024×1024 master icon** built around one generic field-instrument display, viewed perfectly front-on.

- Background/housing: matte near-black graphite.
- A single recessed rectangular display occupies roughly the central 55–60% of the visual mass.
- Display illumination: warm amber/red-orange, **not** saturated emergency red.
- Inside the display:
  - 4–6 short horizontal/segmented sweep elements;
  - one subtle forward directional wedge/arrow;
  - one slightly brighter vertical **marker notch** that hints at MARK without becoming a pin/map icon.
- Beneath the display: one very small, isolated red status LED.
- Very shallow edge light on the graphite housing so it reads as a physical object rather than a flat rectangle.
- No visible phone frame.

### Absolutely no

- readable text;
- numbers;
- fake MHz/kHz/frequency values;
- AM/FM labels;
- antenna;
- speaker grille;
- screws;
- exact button matrix;
- red-display proportions matching a known SB7/P-SB7 product;
- skull;
- ghost;
- pentagram;
- Ouija imagery;
- haunted house;
- fog/smoke;
- waveform-only symbol;
- neon cyan/magenta;
- photorealistic commercial radio.

### Tiny-size rule

At approximately App Store search-result icon size, the viewer should perceive:

> **black field instrument + illuminated sweep display**

not:

> radio station app / voice recorder / ghost game / SB7 photograph.

## Why it could work

It preserves the strongest visual lesson from the current #1 — **instrument recognition** — without copying its literal hardware/photo strategy.

## Why it could fail

Without a ghost or occult cue, a user could read it as a generic scanner/audio tool.

## Cheapest test

Show the icon alone at 60–80 px beside the current leader, Necrophonic, VOX, Spirit Talker, and Spirit Entities. Ask users to categorize each icon. If ours repeatedly reads only as “radio,” add slightly more paranormal context through lighting/atmosphere — **not** a ghost symbol and not copied hardware.

## Kill criterion

If blinded users identify a specific physical spirit-box model from the icon, it is too derivative and must be abstracted further.

# 9. TRUST MESSAGING

## Trust hierarchy

### A. Screenshots

Use only:
- **Works Offline**
- **No Generated Answers**
- **No Speech Recognition**
- **Mic Only When Recording**

Do not put the complete audio-bank explanation in a screenshot. That would turn a conversion asset into a defense brief.

### B. Subtitle

Use:
- **Works Offline**

Do not use:
- `No Generated Answers` in the subtitle. It is too negative/inside-baseball for the highest-visibility metadata slot.
- `No Subscription` in subtitle. It spends indexed/visible characters on monetization instead of product value.

### C. App description

## Claims evaluated

| Claim | Use? | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|---|

## Does transparency undermine paranormal appeal?

**INFERENCE:** Some casual users will prefer a more magical product. That is acceptable. The current market already offers generated words, SLS visuals, and overt horror products. Our commercial niche is better served by **mystery in interpretation, certainty in mechanism**.

The transparency should appear after the first three benefit-led screenshots, not before them.

# 10. PAYWALL COPY

## Store-creative pricing decision

### Do numeric prices belong in screenshots?
**RECOMMENDATION: No.**

Reasons:
- prices can change during launch iteration;
- screenshots are localized assets while price tiers vary by storefront;
- $1.99 in a hero creative can make the product read as disposable/prank-grade before the user experiences quality;
- $9.99 in a hero creative can create commitment friction before the trial is understood;
- Apple explicitly advises avoiding specific prices in the App Store description because the product page already shows pricing and regional values can differ. [A1]
- the stronger store-level claim is **Try a complete session free + no auto-renewing subscription**.

Use exact prices **inside the in-app paywall**, where StoreKit supplies current localized price display.

## Which claim is stronger: No Subscription or No Ads?

**RECOMMENDATION:** Lead with **No auto-renewing subscriptions**, then `No ads`.

Why:
- ads are a known category nuisance, but several serious paid apps already avoid them;
- recurring subscriptions are a larger category-specific trust objection and the current leader uses recurring IAP alongside lifetime access;
- “No auto-renewing subscriptions” is more precise than “No subscription,” because our Tonight Pass is a time-limited non-renewing entitlement.

# 16. CONVERSION PRE-MORTEM

## Failure mode 1 — It looks like a generic audio/radio app

**Why it could happen**  
A waveform, amber screen, and audio controls are generic without enough `spirit box` semantics.

**What we know**  
SBX12 and the current leader get category recognition from device-like control semantics. [W1][B1]

**Mitigation**  
Screenshot 1 must visibly show sweep rate, direction, source-position/sweep activity, fixed instrument controls, and the large MARK/REC controls — not just a waveform.

**Cheapest test**  
One-second category-recognition test at App Store search-result size.

**Kill/redesign criterion**  
Repeated “voice recorder,” “radio,” or “audio analyzer” answers.

---

## Failure mode 2 — It looks like an SB7 clone

**Why it could happen**  
The easiest way to communicate “spirit box” is to borrow the recognizable physical-device grammar too literally.

**What we know**  
The current leader already uses a literal red-screen handheld-device icon and “classic design” creative. [W1][W2] Canonical IP guardrails forbid exact hardware imitation. [C1]

**Mitigation**  
No case silhouette, speaker grille, fake antenna, exact red-screen ratio, copied button grid, or fake frequency data.

**Kill/redesign criterion**  
Blinded users name a specific physical spirit-box product before being told the category.

---

## Failure mode 3 — MARK still does not make sense

**Why it could happen**  
MARK is a verb without a familiar App Store-category convention.

**What we know**  
Paranormal audio users value review/tagging; GhostTube EVP explicitly supports tagging and scrubbable review. [E1][E2]

**Inference**  
MARK is valuable only when screenshot 3 immediately shows the payoff.

**Mitigation**  
Never run screenshot 2 without screenshot 3 directly after it.

**Kill/redesign criterion**  
Users understand “tap MARK” but cannot explain what they get later.

---

## Failure mode 4 — “No Generated Answers” creates doubt instead of trust

**Why it could happen**  
A casual user might ask why generated answers were expected in the first place.

**Mitigation**  
Keep it out of title/subtitle/first three screenshots. Use it only after product value is established in screenshot 4.

**Cheap test**  
Compare screenshot 4 against a positive-only trust variant: `WORKS OFFLINE. BUILT FOR PRIVATE SESSIONS.`

**Kill criterion**  
If “No Generated Answers” systematically creates confusion or lowers product-page conversion in PPO, replace it while preserving full mechanism disclosure in description/onboarding.

---

## Failure mode 5 — Transparency kills the paranormal fantasy

**Why it could happen**  
Saying “short vocal fragments and noise textures” can sound less magical than “real radio” or generated spirit phrases.

**Strong bear case**  
Bello and GhostTube VOX can truthfully market live-radio sources, which are easier to map to the physical spirit-box mental model. [G1][L1]

**Why we still keep transparency**  
The architecture is locked because offline reliability, rights, maintenance, and trust make the bank the better V1 business. [C1]

**Mitigation**  
Lead with the **experience**, not the architecture. Full mechanics come after the first three screenshots.

**Kill criterion**  
If reviews repeatedly reject the fundamental audio source as deceptive despite accurate disclosure and strong sound quality, that is a product-level invalidation, not a copy problem to rationalize away.

---

## Failure mode 6 — The page is too serious for casual users

**Why it could happen**  
No ghost art, no generated words, and a field-tool interface reduce instant spooky spectacle.

**Mitigation**  
Use the warm illuminated display, tangible controls, the question “Hear something?”, and the free real session to preserve excitement without becoming a game.

**Kill criterion**  
If casual test users consistently describe the page as boring/technical and cannot imagine using it tonight, test a slightly more atmospheric background treatment — not feature bloat or fake paranormal output.

---

## Failure mode 7 — The page is too casual for hobbyists

**Why it could happen**  
“Try free” and a $1.99 pass can make the app look like a party novelty if over-emphasized.

**Mitigation**  
Keep price out of store creatives. Make the first four screenshots instrument-first; monetization trust comes last.
